## Problem

What was actually broken, missing, or expensive before this existed? One
paragraph, concrete. Avoid "the client wanted a website."

## Constraints

What genuinely limited the solution — budget, deadline, an existing system that
couldn't change, a platform requirement, a skill you didn't have yet. Real
constraints make the decisions section make sense.

## Decisions

The choices you made and *why*, including the ones you'd defend. This is the
section a technical interviewer will actually read.

## The Struggle

What went wrong, what you got wrong the first time, what took three attempts.
This is the section that makes the rest believable — a case study with no
struggle reads as either trivial or invented.

## Limitations

What it doesn't do, and what you'd change with more time. Naming limits reads as
confidence; claiming none reads as inexperience.
